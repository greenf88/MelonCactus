# Practical AI fictional cases — editorial review

Date: 17 October 2026
Status: draft content for PR #22; not a record of delivered work

## Purpose

Four original fictional scenarios were created to explain how MelonCactus could approach a bounded practical-AI question for an SME. Every public article states that it is fictional and does not describe a MelonCactus client, completed assignment or measured result.

## External pattern review

Official Innoworks material was reviewed on 17 October 2026 to identify broad process categories that prospective clients may recognise:

- case overview: https://innoworks.ai/ai-projecten
- controlled intake and dossier completeness: https://innoworks.ai/case-studies/snelder-zijlstra
- internal knowledge retrieval with visible source context: https://innoworks.ai/case-studies/hypotheekcompany
- authorised meeting transcription and reviewed follow-up: https://innoworks.ai/case-studies/in-het-veld-ai-agent
- practical team workshop: https://innoworks.ai/case-studies/aroundtown

These sources informed topic selection only. MelonCactus does not reproduce the named clients, product architecture, integrations, quotations, numerical results, delivery times or commercial claims from those pages.

## Original MelonCactus scenarios

1. A technical service company tests internal search across an approved document set.
2. An installation company tests a traceable matrix for tender-document review.
3. An industrial maintenance team tests reviewed meeting summaries and action points.
4. A technical wholesaler tests internal structuring of customer requests and missing information.

The scenarios use no invented company names or quantified benefits. Each one distinguishes a proposed workflow from a proven outcome, keeps human decision rights visible and includes privacy, access or retention limits where relevant.

## SEO boundaries

Phrases such as “AI automation for SMEs”, “internal AI knowledge base”, “AI document comparison”, “AI meeting notes” and “customer request intake” are used only where they describe the article. The copy avoids repeated keyword blocks, hidden terms, location lists and claims of completed client work.
